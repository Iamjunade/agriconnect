1. Problem Statement (Official)
Title: Strengthening market linkages and price discovery for farmers
Problem Description (as issued by Government of Maharashtra):
Many farmers, especially smallholders and producer groups, have limited visibility of current and
expected prices across nearby markets, processors, institutional buyers and digital trading channels.
Information on quality specifications, demand, logistics, storage, payment reliability and buyer credentials
may be fragmented. Farmers may sell immediately after harvest because of liquidity or storage constraints
and may have weak bargaining power. Buyers, meanwhile, may struggle to aggregate consistent volumes
and verify quality. The challenge is to improve transparent price discovery and create reliable, efficient
linkages from farm gate to suitable buyers.
Expected Solution / Outcome (as issued):
A market-intelligence and transaction enablement solution that aggregates mandi prices, buyer demand,
quality requirements, arrival volumes, transport and storage options; provides localised price trends and
sale-window recommendations; matches farmers/FPOs with verified buyers; enables lot creation, quality
grading, digital offers, logistics coordination and payment tracking; and supports dispute or grievance
processes. Expected outcomes include improved farmer price realisation, reduced information
asymmetry, lower transaction cost, stronger FPO aggregation, reduced post-harvest loss, more reliable
buyer sourcing and transparent transaction records.
2. Problem in Simple Terms
Think of it from a farmer's point of view:
You've just harvested your crop. You don't really know what price it's fetching at the mandi 20km away
versus the one you always sell at — the information exists somewhere, but not in a form you can quickly
check.
Even if you did know the price, you don't know if it's about to go up (worth waiting) or down (better to sell
now).
You have no cold storage, so you often have to sell fast, even at a bad price — you have no cushion to
negotiate.
You don't know which buyers pay reliably and which don't.

=== PAGE 2 ===
Even if a good buyer exists, you alone might not have enough produce to meet their minimum order — but
you don't have an easy way to team up with nearby farmers who grow the same thing.
If a deal goes wrong (bad payment, quality dispute), you have no formal way to raise it.
Now think of it from a buyer's side (a trader, processor, or institutional buyer):
You need a consistent, verified-quality volume of a crop — but sourcing that means dealing with dozens
of scattered small farmers individually, with no guarantee of quality consistency.
You have no easy way to place a demand ("I need 5 tons of grade-A onions by Friday") and have it reach the
right farmers.
In one sentence: both sides of the transaction are operating with incomplete information and no efficient
way to find and trust each other — and that gap is where farmers lose money and buyers lose reliability.
3. Why This Is Still an Open Problem (Market Gap)
It's important to know this isn't a totally unsolved space — government platforms already exist:
Agmarknet / eNAM already provide live mandi price data and even online bidding.
Private apps (DeHaat, Kisan Suvidha, Khetiyaar, Farmer.in) already show prices and, in some cases,
marketplace features.
So what's actually missing?
Raw prices ≠ a decision. No existing tool tells a farmer "sell here, sell now" — factoring in transport cost,
quality, and price trend, in one clear answer.
Access is still app/web-first. Farmers with low literacy or poor connectivity are underserved — this is
explicitly acknowledged as a persistent gap in eNAM adoption.
No lot-level transaction workflow. Existing tools stop at "here's a price" or "here's a listing" — none carry
a farmer through lot creation → grading → offer → logistics → payment → dispute resolution, which is
exactly what this PS is asking for.
No dynamic aggregation for non-FPO farmers. Formal FPOs solve bargaining power for their members;
there's no lightweight way for any group of nearby smallholders to pool an order on demand.
No transaction transparency layer. Buyer reliability, payment tracking, and grievance handling are not
addressed by any current price-display tool.
Our positioning: Don't rebuild price data (that already exists and works) — build the decision +
transaction layer on top of it that nothing else currently offers.
4. Proposed Solution — AgriConnect
AgriConnect is a market-intelligence and transaction-enablement platform with two entry points:
Farmer/FPO side: WhatsApp or voice-call based (low-literacy, low-connectivity friendly)
Buyer/Admin side: Web dashboard (for buyers, FPOs, and government reviewers/judges)

4.2 What Makes This Different (Innovation Summary)
Decision layer, not just a data layer — net-price calculation + sell/hold advisory, which no current
platform offers together.
Voice-first, not voice-added — designed around a phone call/voice note as the primary interface, not a
translated afterthought.
On-demand aggregation without FPO membership — dynamic clustering of nearby smallholders for any
transaction, not just formal FPO members.
Full transaction lifecycle — lot creation through payment tracking and disputes, directly matching what
the PS explicitly asks for (most competitors stop at "show a price" or "list an item").
Built on government data, not competing with it — uses Agmarknet/eNAM as the price source, making it
realistically deployable with state backing.
